# Concept Statement

As a Canadian, I have asked myself many times in the past what being a Canadian means, what our culture and identity looks like compared to other countries. It's common knowledge that Canada has always been a very multicultural and diverse countries, due to the results of large-scale immigration over hundreds of years, and, early on, this made me worry that we had no unique identity, as a large portion of our population are so ethnically diverse, and each identify with their own nationality with which they emigrated from or were born from. As I grew up and became more culturally aware, I realized that this diversity is what makes up Canada's identity and a great moment of realization for this was my visit to Richmond, British Columbia three years ago. I found it to be such a fun, unique place that felt unlike anywhere I've ever been before. It seriously felt like a slice of Asia within North America, which felt so surreal to me, as someone who has not traveled much outside of Canada in their lifetime. It quickly became one of my favourite places I've ever visited and, to show my love for this city and its awesome diversity and culture, I chose Richmond as the location to base my data visualization on.

I wanted to focus on quantitative data to show how diverse the city is, beyond just identifying what portion of the population is a visible minority (non-Caucasian in race or non-white in colour). With this in mind, I focused on obtaining data showing which cultural identity people resonate with beyond their skin colour, as well as data showing what different languages and religions are observed within the city. Finally, I also managed to show where you can see these examples of multiculturalism within the city, which is almost everywhere, surprisingly. Additionally, I think representing the city's amazing food scene with a data visualization showing how much people in Richmond love the diverse food culture the city has was another great way to show where people can see diversity within Richmond. My call to action for the audience was ultimately drawn from my own experience trying to find where multiculturalism exists in Canada, and I call on the audience to do the same and draw their own conclusions on what it truly means to have a Canadian identity.
