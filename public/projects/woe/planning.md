When you look for multiculturalism around you, where do you see it? It could be the ethnic foods aisle in the grocery store. Or an international student club at your university. Or a cultural festival. Within the city of Richmond, B.C., multiculturalism is engrained within the city's daily life. 

Richmond is an incredibly diverse and multicultural city.
And the demographic data shows it. Statistics Canada and the City of Richmond (2021) reveals the census data for the city and shows that 80.3% of the total population of 208,395 identifies as a visible minority, which is defined as "non-Caucasian in race or non-white in colour" (p. 2). Figure 1 goes into a bit more detail, showing us that Chinese people make up the largest number of visible minorities in the city, with Statistics Canada and the City of Richmond (2021) explaining that they make up 48% of the city's total population (p.1). This diversity in population means so much for the city's cultural development. It means people from all walks of life, from all different backgrounds, with incredibly unique stories and viewpoints on different topics, all coming together  in the beautiful area of Metro Vancouver, to share their experiences and enrich our own Canadian identity. Figure 2 supports this twofold, with more detailed data on who identifies with their certain ethnicities within Richmond. In fact, Statistics Canada and the City of Richmond (2021) gives an example that, within just 25 recorded resident respondents in the City's census, there were over 180 different ethnic or cultural origins (p.1). 

So, geographically speaking, where do we find multiculturalism? In Richmond, it's in every single Urban Planning Region in the city. 12 out of the 14 Urban Planning Regions that make up the city have over 50% of their population as visible minorities, as shown by Figure 6. This means rich cultural events and unique perspectives on almost every block in the city.

The city being very diverse is great, but where can we see examples of multiculturalism specific to Richmond? Religion is a good example of this. Figure 5 shows that, although a very slight majority of Richmond's residents are secular or non-religious, there are many different religions followed by the other half of the city's residents, with Christianity, Buddhism, and Sikhism being the top 3 in numbers. Another great example of Richmond's diversity would be in the City's awesome food scene. Figure 6 shows how 9 of the City's top 10 restaurants on Yelp are locally-run Asian restaurants, as a result of such an ethnically rich and diverse populace.
